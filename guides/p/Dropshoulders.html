What does 240 GSM mean?

GSM is the weight of the fabric in grams per square metre. A higher number means a thicker, heavier fabric. Everyday T-shirts are usually 160–180 GSM; 240 GSM is a heavyweight fabric.

For an oversized drop-shoulder T-shirt the weight matters a lot. Heavier fabric falls straight and keeps the wide, boxy shape instead of clinging to the body. It is also less see-through in light colours like White, Off-white and Beige, and it feels more premium in the hand — which is why many streetwear brands choose 220–260 GSM.

Printing on 240 GSM

  • DTF — works on 100% cotton. We print DTF sheets too: order DTF prints. They come as a separate parcel from the T-shirts.
  • Screen printing — the thick, stable fabric gives a smooth base for plastisol and water-based inks.
  • DTG — direct-to-garment printing gives its best colours on 100% cotton.
  • Embroidery and puff print — heavier fabric holds stitches and raised prints better than a thin tee.

Always test your print on one sample piece before a bulk run.

Which colours suit which print method

  • DTF: every colour, dark ones too, because the transfer carries its own white base.
  • DTG: brightest on white and light colours. Dark colours need a pre-treatment first.
  • Screen printing: every colour. On dark colours plan a white under-base, which adds one screen.
  • Vinyl (HTV): every colour. Pick a vinyl colour with strong contrast.

Terry cotton and bio-wash: what you are buying

The fabric is Oversized Drop-shoulder, 240gsm, Terry cotton/Loopknit Heavy Gauge, 100% Cotton Premium Quality Biowash Fabric. Terry, also called loopknit, is knitted with small loops on the inside and a smooth face on the outside. The loops give the T-shirt its weight and body without making it stiff, and the smooth outside gives your print a clean, even surface.

Bio-wash is an enzyme wash done after dyeing. It takes off the loose fibres on the surface, so the T-shirt feels soft from the first wear and stays smoother after washing.

Drop-shoulder or regular fit?

In a drop-shoulder T-shirt the shoulder seam sits below the wearer's own shoulder, on the upper arm, and the body is cut wide and boxy. That is the relaxed oversized look streetwear buyers ask for, and it gives a wide, flat chest and back for big prints. A regular-fit T-shirt has its seam on the shoulder and a closer body. For a regular fit, see the round neck T-shirts in our catalogue.

How to check a 240 GSM blank before a bulk order

  • Order one sample piece first: it costs ₹245 + GST.
  • Feel both sides: smooth outside, small loops inside.
  • Hold it up to the light: the heavier the fabric, the less you can see through it, which matters most in white and light colours.
  • Stretch the neck rib and let go: it should spring back flat.
  • Wash it once, then measure the chest and length against the size chart above.
  • Print your real design on it before you print the whole run.

Mistakes to avoid when buying 240 GSM blanks

  • Judging by GSM alone. Two 240 GSM T-shirts can feel different: terry (loopknit) and single jersey of the same weight drape differently. Ask what the fabric is.
  • Equal pieces in every size. Most buyers sell more M and L than S or XXL; see the size split above.
  • Skipping the sample. One piece tells you more about fabric, fit and print than any photo.
  • Promising colours before checking stock. Check the live stock for every colour and size before you take your customer's order.

Care tips for your customers

  • Wash inside out in cold water.
  • Dry in shade, not in a hot dryer.
  • Iron inside out, and never iron directly on the print.
  • Wash dark and light colours apart for the first few washes.
